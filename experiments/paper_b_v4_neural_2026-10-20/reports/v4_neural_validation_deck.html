The theory makes three claims; each became a frozen test

1Where the loop goes

The end point depends on (α, β) only through the sharpness B = β/(1 − α); α changes only the approach.

tested by H1, H2 · prop:target, prop:adaptation-horizon

2What it concentrates on

As sharpness grows (full refresh, α = 1), the policy concentrates on the Condorcet winner, or tends to the maximal lottery if there is none. Hard labels leave DPO without a finite target.

tested by H3, H4 · prop:endpoints, cor:sst

3Whether it settles

On a majority cycle the target is stable iff α > (γB² − 1)/(γB² + 1): at the same target, slower refresh buys stability.

tested by H5, H6 · prop:fixed-target, thm:local-spectrum

E1: a judge that favours the current model is hidden refresh

judge bias

Favouring the current model adds βε to the refresh weight: past α + βε = 1 the policy collapses, while a frozen judge keeps a target.

DPO, soft reward-model labels, α = 0.5, β = 1, λ = 0.8, 60 rounds, 64 panels. Statement: Proposition 1 (T1, ours). α + βε and the factor 2.1 = 1 + βε/(1 − α) derived. Why: a few fresh-Adam steps move a fixed distance,
so they overshoot near the target

T6 needs each round to take a fixed fraction of its move; Adam’s first steps take a fixed size instead, so short rounds overshoot when the move is small and the loop never slows down.
