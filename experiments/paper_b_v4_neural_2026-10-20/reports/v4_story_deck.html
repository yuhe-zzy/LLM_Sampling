As B grows, the loop concentrates on a Condorcet winner

Full refresh lets B grow every round: the winner takes all the mass on 100% of panels. Under the logit link, deterministic labels leave no finite target at all. At 3B, full refresh puts 1.000 mean mass on the winner (largest on 100% of panels).

Setup: Qwen2.5-0.5B, 130 panels. (a) IPO, hard Skywork labels, α 1, β 1, λ 1, 40 rounds. (b) α 0.8, β 1, λ 0.8, 60 rounds (first 30 shown). B-E8; Prop. endpoints (Thm 10) and the deterministic-label result of §5.

Pooled judges cycle only through who labels which pair

A cycle needs a covariance between routing and preference. Only on-policy sources feed it back into the loop.

Thm 11 (prop:pooled), identity link, on any triangle T of answers; each criterion alone is transitive, so the first term vanishes. Cor. pooled-onset: the onset step is of order under routing share c and under a uniform mixture (m: standardized gap). Schematic.

Same consistent judges; routing alone makes a strong cycle

No judge changes its mind. Routing asks each judge only about its own top pair (gap +1), never the pair it opposes most (gap −2): who labels a pair covaries with what that judge prefers, which is the Cov term of slide 13.

Bradley–Terry judges with gap m = 1, P = σ(gap); Case 1 averages the three judges on every pair, [σ(1) + σ(−2) + σ(1)]/3; Case 2 gives the pair A, B to rigour, B, C to creativity and C, A to concision. Idealized: three answers that the criteria rank in rotation are rare in sampled answers (X11), so X15 selects such panels and tests what happens when they occur, not how often.

Routing lowers the step at which the loop starts to rotate

Why real data rarely cycle

Mixing consistent judges at random leaves only a third-order cycle: 0.06–3.1% of real panels lose the Condorcet winner (slide 17).

Where the danger comes from

Multi-aspect datasets that pair the best and worst answer per aspect let each criterion pick its own pairs: Case 2.

Detect and repair

D4 shuffles which criterion labels which pair at fixed shares: HelpSteer + concision cycles 0.92 points more than shuffled. Repair: random assignment (X15) or slower refresh at the same B.

Only on-policy sources feed back

A criterion that labels a fixed offline set shifts the target but cannot cause rotation; it must label the model’s new answers each round (Thm 11, part 4).

Cor. pooled-onset (v4 §6): K = 3, uniform reference, λ = 1, actual DPO, the example of the previous slide (m = 1). The onset falls from order m⁻³ under a uniform mixture to order (cm)⁻¹ under routing; the numbers are those of the v4 text (1/β thresholds 0.063 and 0.18).

Replay flips stability; adaptive α and checkpoint serving help

Replay keeps the targets but its effect on stability changes sign with α. The adaptive rule needs no estimate of γ. Weight averaging does not give the average policy.

Three things to take away

  1. 1Refresh sets two separate things.B = β/(1−α) fixes how sharp the target is; α at that B trades speed for stability through α² + γ² < 1.
  2. 2Concentration is the common outcome.Cycles need a covariance between who judges and what they prefer; real and pooled feedback rarely remove the Condorcet winner.
  3. 3The predictions hold in training, and can steer it.Preregistered tests at 0.5B, 1.5B and 3B match the population model; P-free tests chose interventions that kept X14 on target in 7 of 9 arms.
