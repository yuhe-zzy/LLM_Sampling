## Bottom line

The new diagnostic layer is useful, but the available public panels primarily provide **negative controls** for the instability story. A nonzero cyclic residual is common under several constructed preference links, yet its *realized* gain at the fixed point is usually small: directional preferences concentrate the panel policy and suppress the softmax Jacobian. The proposed six-run neural history pilot is predicted to be stable before either intervention is applied. The immediate objective is therefore a **pre-registered discriminating test**, not a broad sweep: establish a coherent cyclic positive control, an otherwise matched incoherent control, and the correct population operator for each training loss.

## Four mismatches to resolve before interpreting a neural result

1. **Actual DPO versus entrywise-logit $\Psi$PO.** [The training code](../cyclic_history/history_math.py) solves the positive pairwise DPO/BT loss, while much of the diagnostic grid treats $\logit(P)$ as its feedback matrix. They coincide in important correctly specified BT cases, but not for general cyclic or heterogeneous $P$. With pair law proportional to $\mu_i\mu_j$, the actual DPO optimum $v^*(\mu)$ satisfies $\sum_j\mu_j[\sigma(v_i-v_j)-P_{ij}]=0$. For a balanced soft-label matrix, uniform $\mu$ and $v^*=0$, implicit differentiation gives $D_\mu v^*=4(P-\tfrac12)$. Thus the actual DPO local gain is based on $4(P-\tfrac12)$, not entrywise $\logit(P)$. As a checked four-response example, take an oriented four-cycle with edge preferences $0.8/0.2$ and opposite pairs at $0.5$. At $\alpha=0.84$, $\lambda=0.8$, `beta_train=1`, the actual DPO local index is **0.936** (stable), while the entrywise-logit proxy gives **1.013** (unstable). This is an exact balanced-case calculation plus a numerical derivative check, **not yet a general DPO theorem**. Hard 0/1 labels make the entrywise logit undefined, although actual DPO can have a finite optimum on a strongly connected cyclic oracle.
2. **Training-label construction versus population grid.** [`hodge/run.py`](hodge/run.py) explicitly skips `single_score_hard` in the population grid, although hard mean-score labels are the historical transitive IPO pipeline's construction. Add this arm; do not read its static $C$ share as if its dynamic frontier had already been tested.
3. **Implementation of sampling extrapolation.** [`build_outer_state`](../cyclic_history/history_math.py) uses the known full $P$ to compute this round's and the preceding round's population-optimal feedback, then shifts a frozen effective reference. In unrestricted population optimization this produces the intended target, but under finite-step LoRA it is **not** the same gradient estimator as the manuscript's negative-weight two-sampler objective. It also requires full-oracle access to $P$. Either test the two-sampler loss directly, with its signed *weights* but nonnegative sampling probabilities, or label the current arm “oracle-assisted feedback extrapolation” and limit the claim accordingly. Do not infer practical data-efficiency or variance properties of one implementation from the other.
4. **Roadmap predictions exceed their theorem scope.** The local frontier establishes local decay/expansion; outside it, generic nonconvergence need not mean a periodic cycle, and inside it is not by itself a global convergence certificate. Equal $\beta\lambda$ does not generally imply equal trajectories: changing $\beta$ also changes the directional forcing $\beta u$ and hence the fixed point. Test gain collapse only on a balanced $u=0$ oracle with matched reference/coverage, then test how it breaks as $u$ grows. Increasing $\alpha$ need not monotonically increase the realized gain because $\pi^*(\alpha)$ can concentrate.

## Recommended sequence and stopping gates

### Gate 1 — CPU-only operator and identification audit; no GPU

- Add actual hard-label IPO to the diagnostic population grid. For DPO, compute the fixed point and local Jacobian of the **actual BT optimizer** alongside the entrywise-logit $\Psi$PO proxy; report both rather than relabeling one as the other.
- Build a controlled four-response **balanced soft cycle**: oriented adjacent edges $P_{ij}=0.8$, reverse edges $0.2$, opposite pairs $0.5$, so $u=0$. Make a coherent arm with the same cycle orientation across prompts and an incoherent arm that permutes only the cyclic orientation across prompts, while preserving each panel's directional component, cycle strength, pair budget, and response support. This isolates cross-prompt coherence from the directional-quality confound in the existing tournament.
- Calculate the *actual* initial Qwen panel probabilities, reference logits, fixed points, local multipliers, and intervention thresholds before selecting neural hyperparameters. Preselect or construct panels with sufficiently interior initial mass; a softmax of raw sequence-sum log probabilities can concentrate heavily even when the oracle is balanced. Do not silently replace it with a relative-to-initial sampler: that would be a different protocol.
- For real vote panels, bootstrap the **frontier index** rather than only cyclic-energy share. Add an injected-cycle recovery/power check or held-out votes; report when a cycle large enough to matter dynamically would be detectable on the observed comparison graph.

**Gate 1 deliverable:** one frozen table per oracle and objective: $u$, $C$, reference and coverage, $\pi^*$, actual versus proxy Jacobian, index with uncertainty, predicted regime and margin, and exact-population trajectories for ordinary, lagged sampling, and lagged reference. The current six-run point fails this gate because its ordinary control is predicted stable.

### Gate 2 — small matched neural pilot, only after protocol review and run approval

- Select one ordinary-unstable case **well beyond** the measured boundary, one ordinary-stable negative control, and the coherent/incoherent cycle pair. Choose $\kappa$ and $\nu$ from the measured objective-specific local gain so the interventions have a predicted stability margin; do not retain `kappa=0.25` merely because it is in the prepared JSON. A population-only design illustration, *if the panel reference and coverage are uniform*, is $\alpha=0.9$, $\lambda=0.8$, the balanced $0.8/0.2$ cycle, and an objective-calibrated gain $\gamma=0.6$: ordinary index is 1.17, while $\kappa=0.5$ and $\nu=0.45$ lie inside their respective local stable regions. This is **not** a locked neural configuration; recompute it using actual panel scores and optimizer response.
- Freeze the token/sequence reduction (currently sequence sums), actual pair law and one-time importance weighting, oracle labels, initial support, reference scores, optimizer/reset policy, inner-step budget, LoRA settings, and seeds. The existing plan has only one seed and two sampled pairs per prompt per round. Use at least five seeds for a claim; a pilot may start smaller but cannot establish a result.
- Compare ordinary / lagged-reference / one explicitly named sampling intervention within each objective, at identical data and compute budgets. Log centered-logit trajectories and cyclic-mode projections (phase, amplitude, spectral peak), step and fixed-point residuals, panel mass/entropy, sampled responses, and held-out quality. The logged `operator_residual` combines finite-sample noise, optimization error, and representation bias; do not call it pure optimization noise. A full-pair versus two-pair and a short inner-budget ablation can separate these causes.

**Gate 2 success:** across seeds, the ordinary coherent arm exhibits the pre-registered dynamical signature, its incoherent or stable control does not, and a predicted intervention suppresses that signature while approaching the same population fixed point and preserving held-out quality within the stated tolerance. For an oscillating ordinary arm, compare the intervention with the computed fixed point, not a nonexistent ordinary endpoint. If only stochastic fluctuations appear, do not attribute the historical DPO anomaly to the deterministic $C$ mechanism.

### Gate 3 — theory only where the experiment needs it

The highest-value near-term theorem is the **actual-DPO balanced-cycle local result** above, including the Section 6 characteristic roots with $\gamma$ calibrated to the actual BT optimizer. For general $P$ and nonuniform fixed points, derive the Jacobian by implicit differentiation of the BT first-order conditions; it need not have the purely imaginary cyclic spectrum of entrywise-logit $\Psi$PO. If neural training damps an exact-population instability, measure an effective inner-update relaxation or the accessible LoRA tangent before extending the theory. The shared-feature/NTK calculations in Claude's handoff remain proposals, not manuscript results.
