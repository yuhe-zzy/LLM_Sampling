Steering the loop without knowing the preference (X14), and the basic dynamics at 3B

2026-10-19 to 2026-10-20. Two preregistered experiments for Paper B, run the same night:

  • X14. Qwen2.5-0.5B on the Azure VM; 9 runs, about 2.1 VM hours.
  • The basic dynamics on Qwen2.5-3B. UNC Sycamore cluster; 5 runs, 2 H100s.

Every number is listed in text/sources.md. Preregistrations are in experiments/2026-10-19-x14-steer/prereg/PREREG.md and experiments/2026-10-19-sycamore-3b/prereg/PREREG.md.

Answer.X14 asked whether an intervention chosen only from tests that do not need the preference matrix P keeps real training from three failures:
  • collapsing;
  • over-sharpening;
  • rotating.
The tests used only the model's own log-probabilities and a few sampled judgments. Mostly yes: 7 of 9 arms passed their preregistered criteria.
  • Collapse. A judge that leans toward the current model collapses the naive loop. Measuring its bias after one round and subtracting it put the loop on its predicted end point (entropy 0.683 and 0.696, predicted 0.683 and 0.694).
  • Over-sharpening. An entropy budget stopped full refresh at the target that meets it (end entropy 0.300 against 0.301), and so did a 36-call census before training (0.297 against 0.298). Naive full refresh fell to 0.031.
  • Rotation at B = 12. A census-chosen refresh weight removed rotation entirely: 0% against 100% oscillating.
Two arms failed, so the preregistered verdict is "not fully supported".
  • At B = 24 the census picked α = 0.95. That replaced rotation (100%) with a slow inward spiral, but 44% of balanced panels still exceeded the oscillation threshold after 80 rounds.
  • One compensation arm missed its convergence threshold by 0.007 nats, a threshold that sits at the optimizer's own noise floor.
The 3B replication passed all five preregistered criteria, and the directional check. On Qwen2.5-3B's own panels, with the same α, β and labels as the 0.5B runs:
  • Converge. Reward-model labels at B = 8 end at entropy 0.968, against 0.968 predicted.
  • Polarize. Full refresh with hard labels puts mass 1.00 on the Condorcet winner on every panel.
  • Oscillate or settle at the same target. On planted cycles at B = 12, α = 0.2 rotates on 100% of balanced panels and α = 0.8 on 0%, both as predicted; α = 0.9 with β = 4 (B = 40) swings slowly on 100%, as predicted.

1What was tested, and why

The setup. Iterated preference optimization trains each model on comparisons of its own answers. The KL reference is refreshed toward the latest model with weight α, and β is the step.

  • Sharpness. The loop aims at a target whose sharpness is B = β/(1 − α). Larger B concentrates more.
  • Rotation. When the preference has a cyclic part with gain γB at the target, the loop settles only if α > αloc = (γB² − 1)/(γB² + 1); otherwise it rotates.
  • Hidden refresh. A judge that adds ε(si − sj) of the current model's log-probabilities to its labels raises the refresh to α′ = α + βε. When α′ ≥ 1 there is no target and the loop collapses.

The diagnostics report proposed P-free tests for these quantities:

  • D5 judges the same pairs before and after one round to estimate ε;
  • D3 is a small judged census that estimates γB before training;
  • the model's own entropy on the training prompts.

Fan asked for experimental evidence that interventions chosen from these tests avoid non-convergence and over-sharpening. That is X14.

The second experiment answers a separate request: show the three basic behaviours on a larger model. Fan put it as "on the same model and dataset, choose different α, β and γ, and see response probabilities oscillate, converge and polarize". Fan allowed 2 H100s on Sycamore. It is a 3B replication of the paper's core runs.

2X14: design

Testbed. The testbed is that of the paper's control experiments:

  • Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct, full fine-tuning;
  • 64 fixed panels of four short answers each;
  • 40 fresh Adam steps per round;
  • one seed per arm.

What the controller may read. It never reads P. It reads only:

  • the model's log-probabilities of the panel answers;
  • sampled judgments, drawn from the run's label source the way a judge would be queried.

Pre-training decisions were fixed in the preregistration from seeded samples. Online decisions were taken by the training script and logged.

3X14: results

3.1 Hidden refresh: one extra round of judging puts the loop back on plan

What D5 saw. It acted in all three arms, with ε̂ = 0.500, 0.614 and 0.640 against true values 0.45, 0.55 and 0.55. All three estimates lie 1–1.7 standard errors above the truth; in the earlier simulation the estimator was unbiased.

Debias arms. Over-correcting by about 0.05–0.06 leaves a slight popularity penalty. The loop therefore ends a little flatter than planned, and exactly where the population recursion with the logged ε̂ says (Figure 1).

Compensation arm. It ends at a finite target, as the theory predicts. That target is sharper than planned, because the anchor is now amplified.

Pass criteria:

  • Debias arms: within 0.05 of the prediction and within 0.10 of the planned 0.638.
  • Compensation arm: within 0.05 of the prediction, and a median last-10-round change of the top-two log-odds of at most 0.05 nats.
  • All three arms end with entropy ≥ 0.3 (S1-c): pass.

3.2 Over-sharpening: an entropy budget and a 36-call census both hold the target

Naive. Full refresh keeps sharpening, to entropy 0.031 after 60 rounds (predicted 0.031).

Switch. The rule fired at round 12 (predicted 13), at sharpness B = 5.5. It then held the loop at that target:

  • end entropy 0.300, against 0.301 for the target at B = 5.5;
  • median KL to the target 0.0002.

Census. It chose B̂ = 5.55 before any training. The fixed-reference run ended at 0.297, against 0.298 predicted.

3.3 Rotation: removed at B = 12; at B = 24 the loop spirals in, too slowly for the preset rule

The census. It flagged 47% of panels at B = 12, against 59% with γB > 1 in truth. It overestimated the largest αloc (0.825 against 0.623), which errs on the safe side.

At B = 12. The resulting α = 0.875 removed rotation: 0% of balanced panels oscillate, median KL to the target 0.0019. The naive α = 0.2 gives 100%.

At B = 24. The naive α = 0.5 rotates on 100% of balanced panels, as predicted. The census asked for α = 0.95, its cap.

  • Against the naive run. The trained model stopped rotating in the sense that matters: its median KL to the target fell from 1.31 to 0.009.
  • The preset criterion. 44% of balanced panels still exceeded the amplitude threshold over rounds 51–80 (median amplitude 0.051 against the threshold 0.054), where the population model predicts 0%. That is a failure of S3-c.

Diagnosis, after the fact. Figure 4 shows that the B = 24 census run is not a sustained rotation:

  • its median KL to the target falls monotonically, 0.157, 0.075, 0.031, 0.018, 0.009 at rounds 10, 20, 40, 60, 80;
  • its detrended amplitude falls from 0.075 over rounds 21–50 to 0.051 over 51–80.

It approaches the target about 2–3 times more slowly than the population recursion (0.0009 at round 80). In both, α = 0.95 makes the approach a slow inward spiral: even the population's own amplitude over the last 30 rounds is 0.021.

The 0.0536 amplitude rule was designed to detect sustained rotation. It also catches a spiral that has not finished. This is a reading after the result; confirming it needs more rounds.

3.4 Training loss as logged

Each outer round minimizes a new objective, so the loss is not expected to fall across rounds.

Per round, the plot shows the excess of the loss over that round's exact minimum, at the first and the last of the 40 inner steps (Figure 5). The reconstruction from the saved panel scores matches every logged first-step loss within 2 × 10−6.

  • Far from the target (naive full refresh, naive B = 24), each round closes most of its gap: median excess 0.23 → 0.015 and 5.6 → 0.021.
  • Near a target, a round usually ends further from its own optimum than it started. This happens in 82–98% of rounds for the settled arms, with end excess about 5 × 10−4 to 7 × 10−3.

This is the inner-optimizer noise floor found earlier (X9): a freshly started Adam takes steps of roughly fixed size, which overshoot small moves. It explains both failures. The compensation arm's last-10 step of 0.057 nats equals the debias arms' 0.058 and 0.049. The extra jitter of the B = 24 census run (per-round step 0.022 against 0.015 at B = 12) slows its final approach.

43B dynamics: design

The question. Do the three behaviours of the paper (a graded target, polarization on a Condorcet winner, and rotation on cyclic preferences) appear in a model six times larger, at the settings where the theory places them?

Model and training.

  • Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct, full fine-tuning, fp32 master weights with bf16 autocast.
  • 40 fresh Adam steps per round at lr 2 × 10−5, as at 0.5B. Micro-batches of 32 sequences with gradient accumulation (the 0.5B micro-batch of 128 ran out of memory); the objective is unchanged.
  • UNC Sycamore, partition h100_all: one H100 80 GB per run, at most two at a time. Peak memory 70.8 GiB.

Panels. The 3B model's own panels, built with the v4 recipe (same prompt pool, K = 4 answers of at most six words, 48 samples per prompt, every answer with initial probability ≥ 0.05). 485 of 600 prompts gave a panel; panels 0–63 are the training set.

  • A first attempt reused the 0.5B panels. The 3B model's initial distribution on them is nearly degenerate (mean top probability 0.94), and the population model predicts no oscillation at all, so nothing was trained on them.

Labels. Skywork-Reward-Llama-3.1-8B scores, soft (σ(gap/T), T = 3.964, the standard deviation of within-panel gaps over all 485 panels) or hard (1[gap > 0]); planted majority cycles with the v4 construction and seed (36 balanced and 28 decisive panels).

Predictions. The exact population recursion from the 3B model's own initial scores on the same panels and labels, frozen with a hash before training (predictions_3b.json, sha256 8e7d0ddd…).

Per prompt. The rule for choosing prompts was fixed before any trajectory was viewed: the first three balanced planted panels of the training set whose four answers are plain English. Figure 7 shows the probability of each answer under all five settings.

Training loss. As for X14, each round's loss is shown as its excess over that round's exact optimum (Figure 8); the reconstruction matches every logged first-step loss within 4 × 10−6.

  • Where a target exists, each round starts closer to its optimum: the first-step excess falls from 0.097 to 7 × 10−4 (conv_rm) and from 0.39 to 1.1 × 10−3 (conv_pl, medians of the last 10 rounds).
  • Where none exists, it stays high: 0.25 every round under full refresh, 3.5 and 0.52 in the two rotating runs.
  • After 40 steps the excess sits at 2 × 10−3 to 8 × 10−3 in every run: the same fresh-Adam noise floor as at 0.5B.

Compute. Five array tasks, one H100 each, at most two at a time: 1 h 37 min, 1 h 39 min and 1 h 37 min for the three 80-round runs, 37 min and 49 min for the 30- and 40-round runs; 6.3 GPU-hours in all, about 72 s per round (the timing job had estimated 59 s).

  • Limits. One seed per run; 64 panels; the panels are the 3B model's own samples, so its targets differ from the 0.5B model's and the comparison with 0.5B is by regime, not by value. bf16 autocast at 3B against fp32 at 0.5B.
