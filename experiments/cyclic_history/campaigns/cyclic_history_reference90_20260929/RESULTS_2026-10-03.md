## Observations

The new trajectories show sustained, large probability excursions with
extended periods of one dominant response, followed by switches to another.
Prompt 251 is particularly clear in both objectives. Prompts 54, 737 and 867
also show repeated dominance changes; 612 and 945 are less regularly patterned.
This is evidence of persistent empirical policy motion, not a proof of a
mathematical limit cycle or convergence to a fixed policy.

Compared with the older Stage B reference arm, the mean within-response
temporal standard deviation over states 51..100 increases substantially:

| Method | Old reference alpha=.9, nu=.45 | Reference90 alpha=1, nu=.9 |
|---|---:|---:|
| IPO | .07341 | .22679 |
| DPO | .07566 | .22557 |

These are probability time-series statistics averaged over four responses and
six prompts, not projected cyclic-mode amplitudes. Average adjacent TV changes
from .07600 to .09957 (IPO), and from .07427 to .08815 (DPO). Fewer leading-answer
switches can coexist with larger excursions because one answer can dominate
for longer before a switch. Do not use switch count alone to infer stability.

Mean relative entropy at state 100 is .28527 (IPO) and .24390 (DPO), compared
with .91099 and .84809 for old reference. Low endpoint entropy does not imply
temporal stabilization; the full curves have repeated drops and rebounds.
Concentration among four fixed responses also does not establish pathological
repeated-token generation: no open-generation evaluation was run here.

Crucially, both alpha and nu changed: old reference was
`.1*s_0 + .45*s_t + .45*s_(t-1)`. The new experiment also removes the initial
anchor. This is not a nu-only ablation and does not identify whether removing
the anchor or increasing lagged weight causes the observed difference. No
alpha=1 ordinary baseline was authorized or added. Findings are descriptive
for one seed and six panels, not population-wide statistical claims.

```text
python -m unittest discover -s . -p test_plot_results.py
```

data for either reference90 probability figure. Public files never contain
raw panel text or credentials.
