The loop: a model is trained on comparisons of its own answers,
round after round

This is how SPPO, iterative DPO and self-play recipes work; the question is what repeating the round does.

Training “on a leash” means a KL penalty that keeps the new model near a reference model. The answers are a real panel from our experiments (Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct). Recipes: paper §1–2.

Three dials set every recipe

Published recipes differ mostly in where they set α: a fixed anchor, replacement by the latest model, or a moving average.

In the paper the leash is a geometric mix: the reference’s log-probabilities are α × the latest model’s plus (1 − α) × the original model’s. Recipe examples from the paper’s §2 (fixed SFT anchors, per-round replacement, EMA).

For each prompt, the judge’s preferences split into scores and a cycle

Scores say who is better overall; the cycle is the part no ranking can explain. They play different roles in the loop.

Schematic. The split is the Hodge decomposition of the prompt’s preference matrix after the link (identity for IPO, logit for DPO). A single reward model gives scores only; juries, mixed annotators and the identity link create a cycle part. Paper §4.

The whole loop fits in one line

xt+1 = α·xt + (1 − α)·r + β·u + β·λ·C·πt
xt: the model’s log-probabilities on the prompt’s answers after round t

Only the cycle part looks at the current model. Remove it, and the loop is a simple average that always converges.

The paper’s exact recursion (MRS-ΨPO) for population preference optimization on a finite set of answers; a constant term β(1 − λ)Cπ0 from the original model’s share of the sampler is folded into the score push here. πt = the current model’s probabilities. Paper Theorem 1.

Coordinate 2, how fast: what α does depends on what you hold fixed

One rule explains both: the loop settles when α² + γ² < 1, where γ is how far one round turns it (step × the cycle at the target). Holding the target fixed, a small α means a big step, and the big step makes it circle.

Share of 32 planted cycle panels still oscillating: trained model (bars), prediction made before training (circles); IPO, on-policy. At B = 12, γ = (1 − α) × 1.96 (median): 1.6 at α = 0.2, 0.2 at α = 0.9. Sources G-H5_*, G-H6_*, T3b confirmed

Slow refresh is safe, but its price grows with the square of the cycle

With ordinary refresh, the best schedule still needs about 2γ² rounds per e-fold: a strong cycle makes a stable approach very slow.

Best refresh weight α = γ²/(1 + γ²), spectral radius γ/√(1 + γ²) (paper Prop. 14). Line: the closed form 2 / ln(1 + 1/γ²); dots: grid search. Source R1 confirmed

New: point the leash one model back,
and 2γ² rounds become γ

Same target, same cost per round: a leash that lags one round damps the rotation, so a strong cycle no longer forces a crawl.

The lag already exists in the paper (Appendix D, Theorem 22: reference πt−1απref1−α) but is compared there at equal α, where it only shows its cost; compared at the same target with each rule’s best α, it accelerates. The gap mirrors gradient descent–ascent against extragradient and momentum methods in games (Mokhtari et al. 2020; Azizian et al. 2020; Ibrahim et al. 2020; Gidel et al. 2019). Sources R1, R2 ours

On our planted cycles, the lagged leash needs 7 rounds where today’s best needs 19

The trained model tracks today’s curve, a little slower, down to its noise floor; one run with the lagged leash would show whether the gap survives.

Every other knob in a pipeline moves one of the two coordinates

So the two coordinates double as a design calculus: read off what a knob changes, then predict what it does; seven of seven predictions held in a trained model.

Each knob is an explicit change of α, β, the reference, the sampler or the link (paper Prop. 15, T1, T5, T6). Tags name the preregistered neural tests; X10 is proposed. Sources: v4, controls and X1/X9 fact sheets confirmed

Could mixing criteria make cycles? Only through how pairs
are assigned to the criteria

The pooled cycle is a covariance between which criterion labels a pair and what it thinks of the pair: first order when pairs are built per criterion, third order under a uniform mix. Coupling lowers the step needed to cycle from order m⁻³ to (cm)⁻¹.

Judges: Bradley–Terry scores (m, 0, −m) rotated, m = 1 in the triangles; owner routing at c = 1. Right: three answers, uniform reference, λ = 1; a fixed-reference loop cycles iff its DPO β is below 4(p̄ − ½)/√3, p̄ the pooled edge; dots checked by simulation (16 of 16). Slow refresh at the same target removes the rotation except for strong cycles. Theory note M1–M5 ours · mixed-criteria report

…but real mixed data rarely meet the conditions

Mixing criteria is not a common source of cycling: real criteria largely agree, and building a pair per criterion concentrates the labels on a few pairs. Cycling would need specialist answers, which our data did not contain.

HelpSteer: 6,690 prompts with four rated responses; UltraFeedback: 54,340. Cycles rise where a criterion runs against quality (concision, simplicity). Training panels: population DPO, B = 10, fixed reference (DPO β = 0.1); the preregistered X11 training stage was therefore not run (about 40 VM minutes, $5, for the measurements). confirmed
