Answer.The paper's theory says that iterated preference optimization with a partly refreshed reference heads for a target fixed by one number, the sharpness B = β/(1−α), and that the refresh weight α only decides whether and how fast that target is reached. A real language model trained round by round with this update followed the theory's population predictions, frozen before each batch, on every main test: four refresh rules with the same B reached the same policy, the plateau was set by B, full refresh concentrated on the Condorcet winner, hard-label DPO collapsed, and planted majority cycles oscillated exactly where the stability condition predicts and settled when refresh was slowed at the same target. What is uncertain is reach rather than fidelity: oscillation was shown only on planted preferences, because natural reward-model panels almost never cycle; the practice-like sampled-pair version failed at 32 pairs per prompt and passed at 512; the oscillation measure was amended twice in the cycle batch, the second time after its results were in; and three sub-predictions could not be tested sharply.

1Question and setting

The loop. Iterative post-training (online DPO or IPO, self-play methods such as SPPO) repeats one round many times: sample answers from the current model, label pairs of answers with a preference oracle, and take a preference-optimization step measured against a reference model. Implementations differ in the reference. Some keep the initial model forever (α = 0), some reset it to the latest model each round (full refresh, α = 1), and some mix the two. The paper (JMLR-B-v4) analyses this loop as a recursion, MRS-ΨPO, and asks two questions: where does the policy go, and does it get there or keep moving?

The recursion. Fix, for one prompt, a panel of K candidate answers and write for the model's log-probabilities of them at round t. With the reference , comparisons sampled from , and a step size β, the exact minimizer of a round's expected pairwise loss is

where is the probability that answer i is preferred to answer j, and the link is for IPO and for DPO. The paper calls this the population map. It is exact only for a learner that can set every panel probability freely and optimizes each round to convergence on the expected loss. A real network shares its weights across all prompts, takes a fixed number of optimizer steps, and in practice sees a finite sample of labelled pairs. Whether the predictions survive those departures is the question of this experiment, and it had to be answered before the predictions are claimed in the paper.

Terms used below. The panel policy is the model's probability over the four panel answers, normalized to sum to one. Panel entropy is its entropy in nats (at most ); lower means more concentrated. The target is the fixed point of the population map. A Condorcet winner is an answer preferred to every other answer with probability above one half. A majority cycle has no such answer: a beats b, b beats c, and c beats a. The maximal lottery is the mixed policy that no single answer beats on average. The population prediction of a run is the exact recursion started from the same initial scores, with the same oracle and hyperparameters.

2The theory under test

Six statements of v4 make predictions that a training run can check. Plainly put: the target depends on α and β only through B; α controls the approach; full refresh makes B grow without bound and concentrates the policy; deterministic labels under DPO's link leave no finite target; and when the preference has a cyclic part, a large per-round step makes the policy rotate around the target instead of settling, which slower refresh at the same target prevents.

4Design and its logic

4.1 Model and panels

The policy is Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct, fully fine-tuned (fp32 weights, bf16 autocast). Prompts are open-ended requests with several good short answers: 65 templated requests for names, slogans, titles, recommendations and descriptions, 100 NoveltyBench curated prompts and 435 NoveltyBench WildChat prompts, each with the instruction to answer in at most six words. For each prompt the initial model draws 48 answers at temperature 1, and the four most probable distinct complete answers form a fixed panel. A panel was kept when every answer had initial panel probability at least 0.05; 130 of 600 prompts qualified.

Why fixed panels. The theory's object is a policy over a finite set of answers. On a fixed panel the panel policy is measured exactly (sequence log-probabilities, then a softmax over the four answers), and the population recursion runs on exactly the same initial scores and oracle. What remains different from the theory is the departure under test: one network with shared weights is trained on all 130 prompts at once, with a fixed optimizer budget per round.

4.2 Preference oracles

  • Soft labels with T = 3.168, half the standard deviation of within-panel reward gaps: a Bradley–Terry oracle, additive under DPO's logit link.
  • Hard labels : what a scalar reward model that compares scores produces. Deterministic and transitive, so every panel has a Condorcet winner, the top-rewarded answer.
  • Jury: the vote share of three reward models (Skywork-8B-v0.2, Skywork-V2-Qwen3-1.7B, GRM-Llama3.2-3B), meant to supply natural cycles. It left only 1 of 130 panels without a Condorcet winner, too few to test cycles, so it serves as a practice check (Section 7).
  • Planted cycle (the preregistered fallback): on each panel a random three of the four answers form a rock–paper–scissors cycle of strength q = 0.3 on top of a tilt Δ, and the fourth answer loses to all by e = 0.3. Half the panels are balanced (Δ = 0.05 < q/2: a majority cycle, maximal lottery on three answers) and half decisive (Δ = 0.2: a Condorcet winner). The planted oracle is a labelled control: the theory's predictions differ sharply between the two kinds on the same model, panels and hyperparameters.

4.3 One round on the GPU

Each round recomputes the panel log-probabilities , forms the reference and the sampler , and minimizes the exact expected pairwise loss in the log-ratios h of the trained model to the reference. is the IPO square loss or the DPO logistic loss with soft labels. The optimizer is Adam, 40 steps at learning rate , restarted every round. In the sampled-pair batches the expected loss is replaced by its empirical version on n ordered pairs per prompt drawn from with labels drawn from , as in practice; in expectation the loss is unchanged, so the population predictions are too.

4.4 Measures

  • Residual: per round and panel, the largest deviation (in nats, after centring) between the trained model's new scores and the population map applied to the model's own current scores. It measures fidelity of a single round, without letting errors accumulate.
  • KL to target: between a run's panel policy and the population target, averaged over panels. Targets are computed from each run's own initial scores.
  • Oscillation amplitude: over the last 30 rounds, for each answer, the standard deviation of its probability after removing a linear trend; the panel's amplitude is the largest over answers. A panel oscillates when the amplitude exceeds 0.0536, four times the noise floor (0.0134) measured the same way on the converged batch-1 runs.

5.1 Fidelity of a single round (H0)

Forty Adam steps put the trained model within a median 0.092 nats of the exact population map per round. In the 15 runs whose policy converges, the median residual is 0.065–0.100. It is larger in the runs that oscillate strongly (0.12 at B = 12, α = 0.2; 0.18 and 0.44 at β = 4, α = 0.9 and 0.95, with q90 up to 1.97), where each round makes a large move, and it is meaningless for hard-label DPO, whose target is infinite. The amplitudes of those oscillating runs still match the population model (Section 5.5), so the larger per-round error does not change the dynamics that the hypotheses are about.

Training loss. The runs logged the loss at the first and the last of the 40 inner steps of every round; the steps in between were not logged. Recomputing the step-1 loss from the saved panel scores reproduces the logged values to a relative error of at most , and it yields two numbers the log lacks: the loss of the trained model after the round, and the exact minimum of that round's objective. Each round has its own objective, because the reference and the sampler move with the policy, so the loss across rounds need not fall. What must converge is each round, to its own minimum (Figures 2 and 3).

Three regimes appear. In the first round every 0.5B IPO run ends 0.09–0.14 above its minimum: 40 steps from the pretrained model do not finish the first round. Where the policy keeps moving, each round starts far from its minimum, by a median of 0.47–3.16 in the four oscillating runs and 0.25 under full refresh, and 40 steps end it 0.006–0.014 above. Near the target a round starts close to its minimum (median excess 0.001–0.014), and 40 fresh Adam steps end it 0.005–0.013 above, so the logged loss falls within the round in only 0–74% of rounds, against all rounds in the oscillating and full-refresh runs. This floor is optimization noise ours: a freshly started Adam takes steps of roughly fixed size even when the gradient is small, and the floor matches the per-round residual of about 0.09 nats in scores. A learning rate that decays within each round would likely lower it; that was not tested.

5.4 Full refresh concentrates; the link decides whether a target exists (H3, H4)

Under full refresh with hard labels the mean mass on each panel's Condorcet winner rises from 0.27 to 0.70, 0.95, 0.9995 and 1.0 at rounds 0, 5, 10, 20 and 40, and entropy falls from 1.140 to 0.000. Every panel ends on its Condorcet winner. The dominance-round corollary could not be checked: with hard labels and on-policy sampling, every worst-case margin of the winner is zero on all 130 panels, the case v4 already names as one where the corollary is silent while concentration still occurs.

At α = 0.8, the same hard labels give opposite outcomes under the two links. IPO levels off at entropy 0.677 against the predicted 0.679. DPO collapses to entropy 0.0073 after a single round, and the median log-odds gap between its top two answers keeps growing, 10, 89, 385 and 508 nats at rounds 1, 10, 30 and 60, despite the anchor to the initial model. With soft Bradley–Terry labels, DPO levels off at 0.388 against 0.385 (KL 0.0005).

The frozen H4 criterion compared hard-label DPO entropy at round 80 with round 30; the runs had 60 rounds and the entropy was already 0.0000 from round 5, so the entropy form is degenerate. The verdict rests on the growing log-odds gap, which is the quantity the theory predicts to diverge.

5.6 Practice-like sampled pairs

With 32 sampled pairs per prompt and round, the same-target test failed its frozen criterion. At α = 0, β = 8 the final policy was at KL 1.07 from the target and still 0.64 when averaged over time. At α = 0.8, β = 1.6 it was at 0.08. The cause is sampling noise multiplied by the step: a full step of β = 8 each round turns label noise into large jumps between nearly deterministic states, while slow refresh averages it. The ratio of logit fluctuations between the two runs was 3.4, against 3.0 predicted by a linear noise model at a fixed target, whose stationary variance scales like (1−α)/(1+α). The link test passed at 32 pairs, and the cycle test was inconclusive, because a threshold calibrated on the noisy S1 runs classified nothing as oscillating although amplitudes matched (0.323 vs 0.317).

With 512 pairs, under criteria written before the rerun, every test passed. The time-averaged policies are at KL 0.0086 (α = 0) and 0.0015 (α = 0.8) from the target, and their fluctuation around it differs by a factor of 10.4, against the factor 9 that the linear noise model gives. The planted cycle oscillates at α = 0.2 with amplitude 0.326 against the deterministic 0.317, nine times the decisive panels' 0.036, and settles at α = 0.9 (0.028).

5.7 A larger model

Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct, on 64 of its own 390 panels with expected labels, passed every frozen criterion: final KL to the B = 8 target 0.0005 (α = 0) and 0.0015 (α = 0.8), pairwise 0.0020; soft-label DPO at 0.382 against 0.381, and hard-label DPO at entropy 0.008 after one round; the planted cycle at α = 0.2 with amplitude 0.307 against 0.306, 32 times the decisive panels, and settled at α = 0.9 (0.027). Residual medians were 0.063–0.097. The frozen predictions (target entropies 0.818 and 0.364, 97% of balanced panels unstable at α = 0.2) were written before the first 1.5B run.

In the hard-label 1.5B run the median top-two log-odds gap went 10, 81, 156, 179, 179 nats at rounds 1, 5, 10, 20, 30. The plateau near 179 nats comes from numerical floors on token log-probabilities, not from a finite target.

6Data quality

One problem in the panels was found while this report was written. The panel builder scored each answer's sampled token sequence, while training re-tokenizes the stripped answer text, and for some answers the two forms get very different probabilities. At training time, 13 of the 130 panels have an answer whose initial probability is below 0.05, and 6 have one below 0.01; in the 64-panel subsets the counts are 8 and 5 (0.5B) and 6 and 1 (1.5B). The interior-support filter therefore holds for the sampled forms, not for every trained panel.

The neural-versus-population comparisons are unaffected, because every population prediction used in the evaluation starts from the same training-time scores as the run. The frozen batch-1 numbers used the builder's scores, which shifts entropies by about 0.02 (the entropy at round 0 is 1.159 under the builder scores and 1.140 under the training-time scores); the verdicts above use the run-consistent predictions, a switch logged in PREREG.md with the first H4 result. v4 states the filter as if it held at training time (Section 9).

7What natural preferences look like

The oscillation results come from a planted control, so how often real feedback has cycles matters for what the paper can claim. Three pieces of evidence say: rarely.

  • Reward-model jury. On the 130 panels, the vote share of three reward models leaves only 1 panel without a Condorcet winner, although the models agree on only 68–75% of pairwise orderings.
  • SPPO's released training panels (PairRM preferences on Mistral-7B-Instruct samples, iterations 1 and 3; 1,200 panels in total): 0 majority three-cycles; the cyclic share of the identity-link payoff is 0.118 and 0.113, against 0.200 for a hard order. read
  • Yu He's earlier Oracle1 runs (single seed): IPO entropy against coincides across α (1.11–1.14 at ), with plateaus near 1.26, 0.94–0.96 and 0.61–0.64 for α = 0.8, 0.9, 0.95; hard-label DPO at α = 0.8 keeps concentrating (entropy 0.44 at round 10, 0.16 at round 80). single seed

Natural channels therefore exercise T1–T5, the concentration side of the theory, and those are the predictions confirmed on unmodified data here. T6 is shown to operate in a real model, but on preferences built to have a cycle.

8Conclusions and judgment

1

In a real model, the end point is set by B = β/(1−α), and α sets only the approach.

Four refresh rules at B = 8 end within KL 0.0076 of each other while a B = 2 control is 0.21 away; plateaus sit within 0.005 nats of the B targets; the approach contracts at about α per round. The same holds at 1.5B and, with enough pairs, with sampled labels.

2

Refresh speed decides stability at a fixed target, exactly as the threshold predicts.

Planted majority cycles oscillate in the predicted configurations, with amplitudes within 0.004 of the population model where they oscillate, and slower refresh at the same B = 12 removes the oscillation. Slower refresh at a fixed target also averages sampling noise, by a factor close to the linear-noise prediction.

3

Concentration is the ordinary outcome.

Full refresh puts all mass on the Condorcet winner, hard-label DPO collapses in one round, and natural reward-model panels almost always have a Condorcet winner. This supports the paper's current emphasis on concentration.

4

What the experiment does not show.

Oscillation under natural feedback; DPO on non-realizable (cyclic) labels, whose population map differs from the logit recursion (your review comment R46); convergence to the maximal lottery as B grows; the sandwich and dominance bounds on a trained model; long-form answers, parameter-efficient fine-tuning, or models beyond 1.5B.

Verdict by the preregistered rule. H1–H4 pass as frozen. H5 and H6 pass under a measure that was amended twice, after 2 of 7 batch-2 runs and again after all of them, each time for a stated, documented artifact and with thresholds calibrated only on batch-1 data; the original top-switch measure would have reported false oscillation on stable near-tied panels. The practice-like same-target test failed at 32 pairs and passed at 512 under criteria set after the failure but before the rerun. H0 is met wherever the policy converges. The 1.5B replication passes with no change.

Confidence. High that a full-fine-tuned small model on fixed panels realizes the population map closely enough for every qualitative and most quantitative predictions of v4: the agreement is at the level of individual trajectories, not only end points (Figure 10). Moderate for practice: the regime is short answers on fixed four-answer panels, and the sampled-pair version needs many comparisons per prompt and round before the theory's deterministic predictions are visible.

10Costs and conduct

All training ran on the project VM psipo-h100-1 (one H100 NVL 94 GB, $6.98 per hour), started on 2026-10-16 at about 17:20 UTC and deallocated on 2026-10-17 at about 06:35 UTC: about 13 hours, about $90. No paid API was used. 37 training runs: 2 pilots, 19 main runs, 10 sampled-pair runs and 6 runs at 1.5B. All runs, logs and the VM backup are in results/; the timestamped preregistration log is prereg/PREREG.md.

Limits.

  • One seed per configuration. Agreement across 130 or 64 panels per run is strong, but panels share one network, so they are not independent replicates.
  • Fixed four-answer panels of short answers. The theory's object is a finite candidate set; open-ended generation, where the support itself moves, is not tested.
  • Full fine-tuning only, at most 1.5B parameters, 40 Adam steps per round at one learning rate.
  • The oracles are a reward model and a planted matrix; no human preferences. The jury's cycle count depends on three particular reward models.
  • The oscillation measure was amended twice (Section 4.6), the second time after all batch-2 results were in; thresholds came from batch 1, but both decisions to amend were made after seeing batch-2 runs.
  • Three sub-predictions were scored after the fact; the sandwich could not be resolved and the dominance round did not apply.
  • About 10% of panels have an answer below the intended 0.05 initial probability at training time (Section 6).
  • Only the first and the 40th inner-step losses of each round were logged; the loss curve within a round is not available.
  • Cost and timing are approximate, from session notes.
