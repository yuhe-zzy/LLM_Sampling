Do mixed preference criteria make iterated preference optimization cycle?

2026-10-19. A theory note, a probe of two public multi-aspect datasets, and the measurement stages of the preregistered experiment X11, run on the 130 v4 panels and on 25 new style panels. VM: about 40 minutes of one H100, about $5; no training run. Code and data: reviews/2026-10-19-mixed-criteria-cycles/ and experiments/2026-10-19-mixed-criteria/. Every number below is in reviews/2026-10-19-mixed-criteria-cycles/shared/sources.md.

Answer.Each criterion on its own is a single score per answer, so it cannot rank answers in a cycle. Such criteria include an aspect rating, a rubric and a reward model. Pooling data labelled under several criteria can put a cycle into the labels, and we asked whether it does so often enough to make iterated preference optimization cycle.

The theory gives a clean answer.
  • The pooled cycle is exactly a covariance between which criterion labels a pair and what that criterion thinks of the pair.
  • It is first order in the judges' margins when the choice of pairs depends on the criterion, and third order when the criteria are mixed uniformly.
  • It makes the loop rotate only when it removes the Condorcet winner and the loop takes large steps. In a three-style example, coupling lowers the step needed to cycle from order m⁻³ to (cm)⁻¹, and slower refresh at the same target removes the rotation.
The data do not show such cycles.
  • Pairs built per criterion contain a directed cycle on only 0.6–3.0% of prompts in HelpSteer and UltraFeedback (5.6% with two style criteria).
  • On our training panels, neither measured criteria nor rubric-conditioned judges remove a Condorcet winner. Population DPO therefore predicts no oscillation, and the preregistered training stage was not run.
Mixed data are thus not a common source of cycling. They become one only when the candidate answers include specialists, each favoured by a different criterion, and the pairs are built per criterion. The theory states these conditions precisely, and our data did not meet them.

1Question and setting

Your question (translated): when post-training data from several sources are mixed, with different pair-sampling strategies and different criteria behind the labels, does the mix put cycles into the preferences? Writing is the example. In one context rigour matters more than creativity, in another creativity more than concision, in a third concision more than rigour. If such data are pooled without the context, do generations rotate between styles? A natural scenario like this would show that cycling is less contrived than our planted panels suggest.

Why it matters for the paper. The paper describes the iterated loop by two coordinates.

  • Where the loop goes. This is the target, set by the sharpness B = β/(1 − α).
  • Whether it gets there. The target is locally stable when α² + γ² < 1, where γ is the rotational gain of the labels' cyclic part at the target.

Rotation therefore needs a cyclic part. A single scalar judge has none. The paper's natural-feedback checks (a three-model jury on our panels, PairRM on SPPO panels) found almost no candidate set without a Condorcet winner. Pooling several criteria is the most plausible remaining source of cycles in practice.

2Concepts

Sources and routing. A source is anything that labels comparisons by its own criterion: an aspect rating, a rubric, a reward model, or a data subset with its own guideline. A labelled comparison of answers i and j comes from source k with weight wk(ij). This weight is the source's share of the data times how often the source samples, keeps or is assigned that pair, normalized over sources. We call this assignment the routing. The pooled label is the routed mixture P̄ij = Σk wk(ij) Pkij.

  • Pair-independent routing. The weights are the same for every pair. This is the soft mixture: each comparison goes to a random source, or each pair is labelled by all sources.
  • Pair-dependent routing. The weights depend on the pair. Examples are the multi-aspect binarization that builds a best-versus-worst pair under each aspect, margin filtering (each source keeps the pairs it is sure about), and a judge that decides on the most salient difference.
  • Transitive source. After the link, its payoff is a difference of scores, Akij = ski − skj. Every single criterion and every scalar reward model is of this kind; under the logit link it is a Bradley–Terry source.

Cyclic part and Condorcet winner. The paper splits the payoff matrix A = ψ(P) into a score part and a cyclic part C (Hodge decomposition). Two quantities measure the cycle:

  • The cyclic sum of a triangle (a, b, c) is Aab + Abc + Aca. C = 0 exactly when every triangle has cyclic sum 0.
  • The cyclic share is the fraction of the squared payoffs that lies in C.

A Condorcet winner beats every other answer with probability above one half. It matters for the dynamics through the paper's prop:endpoints: at high sharpness the target concentrates on a Condorcet winner and the gain γB goes to 0. A cycle among the losers then cannot make the loop rotate.

Statements and proofs are in theory.md. Every claim was checked numerically by analysis/theory_mix.py and analysis/onset_scaling.py.

M1 (covariance identity)With the identity link, every triangle T satisfies

  • Two ingredients are needed. The cyclic sum needs both pair-dependent routing and disagreement between sources on the same triangle. By Cauchy–Schwarz it is at most three times routing heterogeneity times disagreement.
  • Two sources. The cycle is the cyclic part of δ ∘ (A¹ − A²), where δ is the deviation of source 1's weight from its mean. The disagreement A¹ − A² is itself a score difference, so a cycle appears only where the routing varies along it.
  • Check. Exact to 3e-16 on 200 random cases with 2–5 intransitive sources.

  • Check. Within 1.5% of 20,000 simulated assignments in 5 cases.
  • Caveat. In an iterated loop that draws fresh labels every round, random assignment averages out to the soft mixture. The variance term is then per-round label noise, not a persistent cycle. Only systematic routing, which depends on what the pair contains, leaves a cycle in the expected labels that the loop follows.

M4 (the strongest cycle routing can make)If the routing on each edge is free, the largest cyclic sum on a triangle is Σe maxk Ake, attained by routing each edge to its most favourable source.

For the three judges of Figure 1, at m = 0.5 / 1 / 2:

  • owner routing gives pooled edges 0.62 / 0.73 / 0.88, as strong as each judge's own margin;
  • salience routing gives 0.73 / 0.88 / 0.98, in the opposite direction;
  • the soft mixture gives 0.505 / 0.527 / 0.593.

With only two criteria, hard salience routing is the sign of the summed gaps, which is transitive.

M5 (dynamics)

  1. Equivalence. Suppose every source samples pairs from the current policy and then keeps or labels them by its routing rule, at a total rate that does not depend on the pair. Then the population round is the paper's round with the single label matrix P̄. Every result of the paper applies with P* = P̄; for DPO this is T1's weighted Bradley–Terry projection.
  2. Only on-policy sources feed back. With per-source sampling levels λk and shares ρk, the rotational gain is that of Σk ρk λk Ck at the target. A cycle that lives only in offline data shifts the target and cannot make the loop oscillate.
  3. Onset in closed form. Take a balanced cycle on three answers with uniform reference and λ = 1. A fixed-reference loop oscillates iff βDPO < 4(p̄ − ½)/√3, where p̄ is the pooled cycle edge.
    • Soft mixing of the three judges gives p̄ − ½ ≈ m³/24.
    • Owner coupling c gives p̄ − ½ ≈ c·m/4 once that term dominates.
    • Coupling therefore lowers the step needed to cycle from order m⁻³ to order (cm)⁻¹.

Away from the balanced point, the paper's local criterion still decides. Figure 4 starts the three-judge example from an SFT-like model that leans to rigour, (0.5, 0.3, 0.2), and compares three recipes with the same target sharpness B = 10. The local modulus at the actual DPO fixed point agrees with simulated oscillation in all 99 cases (3 recipes × 3 margins × 11 couplings).

What the theory predicts for data.

  1. Pooled cycles grow with disagreement between criteria. Quality aspects that agree should give almost none. Adding a criterion that runs against quality, such as concision, should give more.
  2. A cycle in pooled data needs coverage of a triangle. Constructions that put several criteria on the same pair make contradictions, which have no cyclic part, rather than cycles.
  3. In the loop, rotation needs a pooled matrix without a Condorcet winner. Uniform mixing of a few agreeing criteria will almost never produce one. Among four answers scored by three i.i.d. criteria, systematic routing leaves no Condorcet winner on 3–4% of panels; static random assignment does so on 33% (Figure 6).

Readings

  • Disagreement drives the cycles, as M1 says.
    • HelpSteer's quality criteria agree strongly (mean Kendall τ 0.61–0.76), and HS-Q gives cycles on 0.06–0.09% of prompts.
    • Concision runs against them (τ −0.29 to −0.38), and adding it raises C3 to 1.42%; adding simplicity too raises it to 3.14%.
    • UltraFeedback's four aspects agree at τ 0.48–0.58 and give 0.55–1.40%.
  • Coupled construction gives fewer cycles than random pairs, not more. With correlated criteria, best versus worst often picks the same pair for several criteria.
    • About half the C3 prompts end with at most two distinct pairs (HS-S 51.6%, UF 49.4%). Two pairs cannot form a cycle.
    • Where criteria disagree on such a pair, the result is a contradiction, which has no cyclic part. Contradictions occur on 27.8% of HS-S prompts.
    • Among prompts with at least three distinct pairs, C3 cycles on 2.93% (HS-S) and 1.09% (UF).
  • The probe's own hypothesis is refuted. The plan stated that coupled per-criterion construction would make cycles common, and it does not. The disagreement pattern agrees with M1, but that reading was made after the data, so it is post hoc.

5Training panels: the measurement stages of X11

Design. X11 asks the dynamic question on the panels we train on.

  • Panels. The v4 panels: 130 prompts, each with four short answers from Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct and every answer's initial probability at least 0.05.

Results

  • Stage 0. The cyclic part rises from 0.017 (soft) to 0.079 (salience) and 0.122 (filtered). A majority three-cycle appears on 1.5% and 3.1% of panels, and no Condorcet winner on 1.5%. Predicted oscillation is at most 1.5% under every recipe, matching the i.i.d. baseline of Figure 6.
  • Stage 1. The rubric contexts barely move the judge. Its rankings agree with the plain prompt at τ 0.84 (rigour), 0.78 (concision) and 0.44 (creativity). No pooled panel lacks a Condorcet winner, and predicted oscillation is 0%.
  • Stage 1b. 25 of 600 prompts gave a style panel: 470 failed the probability floor of 0.01, because an answer written to a style instruction is improbable under the plain prompt. The contexts now disagree, but the answers are not specialists.
    • Each context's top answer is the one written in its style on 16% (rigour), 48% (creativity) and 32% (concision) of panels, against a chance rate of 25%.
    • No panel has all three, and no pooled panel lacks a Condorcet winner.
    • Predicted oscillation is 0%.
  • Decision, by the preregistered rule. No stage predicts oscillation on 8 or more panels, so stage 2 (training) was not run. The planned runs would have cost about 1.7 VM hours and could only have confirmed a predicted null.

7Conclusions and judgment

1

There is a clean theoretical result, and it belongs in the paper.

Pooling individually transitive criteria makes a cycle exactly through a covariance between routing and disagreement. The cycle is first order when the choice of pairs depends on the criterion and third order under uniform mixing. It feeds the loop only through on-policy, systematic routing. It makes the loop rotate only when it removes the Condorcet winner, with an onset step in closed form, and slow refresh at the same target cures it. This turns the paper's annotator-mixture statement (prop:cyclic-sources) from "almost every mixture has some cycle" into "which mixtures make a cycle that matters, and how large it is".

2

Mixed data are not the missing common source of cycling.

In two large public multi-aspect datasets, per-criterion pairs contain a directed cycle on 0.6–3% of prompts, and on 5.6% with two style criteria pulling against quality. On our training panels, three kinds of mixed sources never removed a Condorcet winner beyond 1.5% of panels. The data therefore do not show that cycling is less contrived than the planted panels suggest. They show why it is rare: real criteria largely agree, and coupled construction concentrates labels on a few pairs.

3

The theory says where cycling would come from, and how to check for it.

Cycling needs three things together: candidate sets with specialists, where different criteria favour different answers; pair construction or judging coupled to the criterion; and a fast on-policy loop, for example a fixed reference with βDPO = 0.1. Each can be measured before training: the disagreement τ between criteria, the coupling of the routing, and a Condorcet-winner check per prompt. Our 0.5B model's style answers were not specialists. Stronger generators with long-form answers might be; that remains untested.

Your question, "is this a strong supplement?" Yes, for the theory and the honesty of Part II, but not as evidence that cycling is common. I would add the following:

  1. A proposition "Pooled sources" next to prop:cyclic-sources, containing M1, M2 and the on-policy part of M5, with M3, M4 and the proofs in the appendix.
  2. One corollary with the onset step and the m⁻³ versus (cm)⁻¹ scaling.
  3. One paragraph in §8 (natural feedback), after the jury and PairRM numbers. It gives the HelpSteer and UltraFeedback rates and notes that rubric-conditioned judges on our panels left every Condorcet winner in place.

The paper's framing would stay as it is: cycling is a property of fast loops whose labels have a substantial cyclic part at the top, and natural or mixed feedback rarely supplies one. A neural style-rotation demonstration would need specialist panels, which we could only build by planting.

  • Cost. VM: two measurement passes, 12:55–13:07 and 13:12–13:36 EDT, about 40 minutes and $5, deallocated after each. No training run.
    • Downloads, approved: HelpSteer (16.7 MB) and UltraFeedback (323 MB) from Hugging Face. Raw files were kept in the session scratchpad as untrusted data and read with isolated Python; sha256 hashes are in realdata/README.md.
    • The probe was built by a subagent and spot-checked: cycle detection was checked against brute force and the sanity constructions.
  • Preregistration. X11 was preregistered before stage 1; stage 1b was added as a logged amendment before it was measured.
    • One wording error in the stage 0 summary was corrected and logged: three-cycle shares had been called Condorcet-winner shares.
    • The probe's plan was fixed before the data. Its deviations (duplicate responses, unparsable ratings, a tolerance on thresholds) are logged in its README.
  • Scope of the probe. It uses the datasets' own responses, not on-policy samples. It pools within a prompt, not across prompts, so style shared across prompts is not tested here; the 10-14 latent study covers that in simulation. HelpSteer's 0–4 ratings tie often: almost every C3 prompt needed a random tie-break, and every cyclic HelpSteer prompt did.
  • Scope of the panels. The judges are scalar reward models with a rubric sentence in the prompt, not pairwise LLM judges. The answers are short (at most eight words) from a 0.5B model. The style panels are few (25), and their probability floor (0.01) is lower than the v4 floor (0.05).
  • Scope of the theory.
    • The onset formula is exact at a balanced three-answer cycle with uniform reference and λ = 1.
    • Elsewhere the local modulus decides; it was checked on 99 cases of one family.
    • M3's variance formula assumes assignments independent across pairs.
    • The i.i.d. baseline is a model of unrelated criteria, not of any dataset.
