# Latent preference framework: preferences as a skew bilinear form on feature space

Date: 2026-10-13. Written by Claude for Fan Yao. Companion script: `latent_check.py` (NumPy, Codex-runtime interpreter). Status: derivation by Claude; refereed adversarially by an independent Opus agent (`referee_report.md`, 2026-10-13): the algebra of L1–L3 was confirmed, one matrix in L3(iii) and one identifiability claim were corrected, and the first numerical run of Section 5(3) was found to have a stalled inner solver, which was replaced and rerun. Items marked [ref] record the fixes.

## 0. Summary

Annotators label concrete responses, but what they reveal is a preference over the *features* of responses. The framework makes this literal: a latent preference is a skew-symmetric bilinear form Ā on a feature space, and the flow between two concrete responses is φ(y)ᵀĀφ(y′). Consequences, all compatible with the manuscript's theory:

1. **Reciprocity is automatic** (skew bilinear forms are antisymmetric), and the manuscript's symmetric-link setting is exactly the case of a general, possibly non-transitive, latent form. The latent Hodge decomposition splits Ā into a latent potential (the intercept row) and a latent cycle (the attribute block).
2. **Each prompt sees a compression.** Prompt a's panel flow is A_a = Φ_aĀΦ_aᵀ. Its Hodge potential is the latent flow against the panel centroid, u_a = Φ_aĀφ̄_a, and its cyclic residual is the compression of the latent cycle onto the centered features. A two-response panel is blind to any latent cycle (C_a = 0); random K-response panels in a 3-dimensional attribute space see on average 30% (K=3), 51% (K=4), 67% (K=6) of a pure latent cycle's energy.
3. **Sparse pairs identify the latent form jointly.** Labeled pairs from any prompts identify Ā as soon as the skew parts of φ_iφ_jᵀ over the pooled pairs span the m(m−1)/2-dimensional space of skew matrices; with dense features 60 prompts with one pair each identify a 4×4 Ā to machine precision. With pure one-hot role features only the observed role pairs are identified, and with role-plus-intercept features the cycle block and the latent potential are confounded (each observation is Ā_rs + ū_r − ū_s) [ref]. This complements the manuscript's Appendix D: per-prompt residuals of sparse panels are not identified, the latent form restricted to the span of the pooled features is. Pooling across prompts is one way to reach that span; a single prompt with m or more generic responses is another [ref].
4. **Latent dynamics.** For a policy linear in latent features, x_a = Φ_aθ, result X5 applies with B_a = Φ_a, and the effective cyclic operator takes the latent form S = Σ_a w_a G_a Ā_cyc G_a with G_a = Φ_aᵀJ_aΦ_a the per-prompt latent Fisher matrix; the Jacobian is (G+R)⁻¹(αG + gS) with G = Σ w_a G_a. When all prompts share one latent Fisher G, the local spectrum is that of αI + gGĀ_cyc: the manuscript's master-recursion spectrum in latent coordinates, with G in the role of J* and Ā_cyc in the role of C.
5. **A latent cycle is necessary but not sufficient for shared instability.** Each panel contributes the induced potential Φ̃_a(ū_lat + Ā_cycφ̄_a) to the pooled forcing, and shifting the attribute origin by t changes ū_lat into ū_lat − Ā_cyc t while leaving Ā_cyc fixed, so panel centroids and latent potentials are the same object [ref]. Unless the aggregate Σ_a w_a Φ̃_aᵀu_a vanishes, the shared policy concentrates and the cycle is damped; even with zero induced potential, a strong enough cycle (large β) drives the policy to concentrate rather than cycle [ref: corrected numerics]. This is the latent form of result X3 and of the manuscript's endpoint dichotomy.
6. **Partial sharing is the analogous construction** with latent features [shared attributes; group indicator ⊗ attributes] and a ridge on the second block; the ridge adds ρP to the Hessian of Theorem L3 (not covered by L3 as stated), which is exactly the penalized Hessian of the partial-sharing theorem (see `../partial-sharing/`) [ref].

## 1. Definitions

- Feature map φ: responses → R^m, with coordinates (φ_attr, 1): m−1 attribute coordinates and an intercept. Features may be hand-built (length, style tags, generating model) or learned.
- **Latent preference.** A real skew-symmetric Ā ∈ R^{m×m}. The transformed flow between responses y, y′ (same prompt) is A(y, y′) = φ(y)ᵀĀφ(y′), and the preference probability is P*(y ≻ y′) = Ψ⁻¹(A(y,y′) + Ψ(½)) for the manuscript's symmetric link Ψ (logit: Ψ(½)=0; identity: scale so that entries stay in [0,1]).
- **Latent Hodge split.** Write Ā = Ā_cyc + (ū_latcᵀ − cū_latᵀ) where c = e_m (intercept coordinate), ū_lat ∈ R^{m−1}⊕{0} is the intercept column of Ā restricted to attribute coordinates, and Ā_cyc is the attribute block (skew, (m−1)×(m−1), padded with zeros). This is unique: the intercept row/column of a skew matrix is exactly a rank-2 "potential" form, the attribute block the rest.
- **Panels.** Prompt a has K_a candidate responses with feature matrix Φ_a ∈ R^{K_a×m} (rows φ(y)ᵀ), centroid φ̄_a = (1/K_a)Φ_aᵀ1, and centered features Φ̃_a = Π_aΦ_a (Π_a = I − 11ᵀ/K_a). Induced panel flow A_a = Φ_aĀΦ_aᵀ.

## 2. Proposition L1 (compression)

For every prompt a: (i) A_a is skew-symmetric, so P*_a is reciprocal for every symmetric link. (ii) Its Hodge potential and cyclic residual (manuscript eq. hodge-components) are
  u_a = Φ_a Ā φ̄_a,   C_a = Φ̃_a Ā Φ̃_aᵀ = Π_a Φ_a Ā Φ_aᵀ Π_a.
(iii) If K_a = 2 then C_a = 0. (iv) C_a depends on Ā only through Ā_cyc: C_a = Φ̃_{a,attr} Ā_cyc Φ̃_{a,attr}ᵀ (centering removes the intercept coordinate), and the potential has the closed form u_a = Φ̃_{a,attr}(ū_lat + Ā_cyc φ̄_{a,attr}) [ref: verified to 10⁻¹⁵]: the latent potential plus the latent cycle evaluated at the panel's attribute centroid, pushed through the centered features.

*Proof.* (i) (Φ Ā Φᵀ)ᵀ = Φ Āᵀ Φᵀ = −Φ Ā Φᵀ. (ii) u_a = (1/K_a)A_a1 = Φ_aĀ(Φ_aᵀ1/K_a) = Φ_aĀφ̄_a. The residual is A_a − (u_a1ᵀ − 1u_aᵀ); since A_a1 = K_au_a and 1ᵀA_a = −K_au_aᵀ, one checks Π_aA_aΠ_a = A_a − (1/K_a)(A_a11ᵀ + 11ᵀA_a) + (1/K_a²)11ᵀA_a11ᵀ = A_a − u_a1ᵀ + 1u_aᵀ + 0 (the last term vanishes because 1ᵀA_a1 = 0 for skew A_a), which is C_a; and Π_aΦ_aĀΦ_aᵀΠ_a = Φ̃_aĀΦ̃_aᵀ. (iii) For K_a = 2, Π_a has rank 1, so Φ̃_a = v φ̃ᵀ for a vector v ∝ (1,−1), and Φ̃_aĀΦ̃_aᵀ = (φ̃ᵀĀφ̃) vvᵀ = 0. (iv) Centered features have zero intercept coordinate (the intercept column of Φ_a is 1, killed by Π_a), so only the attribute block of Ā enters C_a. ∎

Remarks. (a) The per-prompt cyclic energy share ‖C_a‖_F²/‖A_a‖_F² measures how much of the latent cycle the panel exposes; it is zero for pairs and grows with the panel's spread around its centroid relative to the centroid itself (Section 5). (b) Gauge [ref]: translating the attribute coordinates by t maps (ū_lat, Ā_cyc) to (ū_lat − Ā_cyc t, Ā_cyc) and leaves every A_a unchanged; panels enter only through ū_lat + Ā_cyc φ̄_a, so a panel offset is indistinguishable from a prompt-specific latent potential.

## 3. Proposition L2 (identifiability from pooled sparse pairs)

Let the observations be flows a_k = φ_{i_k}ᵀĀφ_{j_k} on labeled pairs k = 1..n drawn from any prompts (one pair per prompt allowed). Write M_k = (φ_{i_k}φ_{j_k}ᵀ − φ_{j_k}φ_{i_k}ᵀ)/2 (the skew part). Then a_k = ⟨Ā, M_k⟩_F, so Ā is identified (uniquely determined by the noiseless observations) iff span{M_k} = Skew_m, the m(m−1)/2-dimensional space of skew matrices; otherwise only the restriction of Ā to span{M_k} is identified. With noisy labels, estimation is a generalized linear model in the skew parameters (logistic for the logit link, least squares on labels for the identity link); consistency needs the population condition E[vec(M)vec(M)ᵀ] ≻ 0 on Skew_m, not merely a spanning sample [ref]. In particular: (a) for pure one-hot role features (no intercept) M_k = (E_{rs} − E_{sr})/2 for the observed role pair (r,s), so exactly the observed role pairs are identified; with the intercept coordinate added, each observation equals Ā_rs + ū_r − ū_s and the cycle block is confounded with the latent potential unless some pairs vary the attribute coordinates continuously [ref]; (b) for features in general position, any n ≥ m(m−1)/2 pairs identify Ā, whatever their distribution over prompts, including all from one prompt with K ≥ m generic responses.

*Proof.* Bilinearity: φ_iᵀĀφ_j = tr(Āᵀφ_iφ_jᵀ)... = ⟨Ā, φ_iφ_jᵀ⟩_F = ⟨Ā, M_k⟩_F because Ā is skew and the symmetric part of φ_iφ_jᵀ is orthogonal to skew matrices. The map Ā ↦ (⟨Ā,M_k⟩)_k is linear on Skew_m; it is injective iff the M_k span Skew_m. (a) and (b) are immediate (in (b), the skew parts of m(m−1)/2 generic rank-two matrices are linearly independent, a Zariski-open condition). ∎

Consequence. Sparse labeling (K_a = 2 per prompt) makes every per-prompt residual zero and unidentifiable, yet the latent cycle Ā_cyc (restricted to the span of the features) is identified as soon as the pooled pairs are rich enough; a shared preference/reward model trained on such pairs carries Ā_cyc and evaluates it on every response the policy samples.

## 4. Theorem L3 (latent dynamics of a shared policy)

Standing hypothesis (H1) [ref]: G = Σ_a w_a Φ_{a,attr}ᵀJ_aΦ_{a,attr} ≻ 0 at the points considered, equivalently the stacked centered features have full column rank (no attribute direction is invisible to every panel). Policy class: x_a(θ) = Φ_{a,attr} θ (centered logits on panel a; the intercept coordinate is a gauge and is dropped), θ ∈ R^{m−1}. Per-prompt targets z_a = F_a(x_a) with F_a the manuscript's master recursion for the panel data (u_a, C_a) of Proposition L1 (coverage term kept). Restricted round: θ ← argmin Σ_a w_a KL(softmax(Φ_{a,attr}θ) ‖ softmax(z_a)).

(i) [Jacobian; X5 with B_a = Φ_{a,attr}] At a fixed point θ* with penalized Hessian 𝓗 = G + R ≻ 0, where G = Σ_a w_a G_a, G_a = Φ_{a,attr}ᵀJ_aΦ_{a,attr} (latent Fisher of prompt a), R = Σ_a w_a Φ_{a,attr}ᵀT_a Φ_{a,attr} (residual curvature), the strict-local-minimizer branch has
  DΦ(θ*) = (G + R)⁻¹ (αG + g S),   S = Σ_a w_a Φ_{a,attr}ᵀ J_a C_a J_a Φ_{a,attr} = Σ_a w_a G_a Ā_cyc G_a.
(ii) [Latent spectrum] If R = 0 (realizable fixed point) the multipliers are α ± igω̃_j, with ±iω̃_j the eigenvalues of the real skew matrix S_eff = G^{-1/2}SG^{-1/2}; the fixed point is linearly stable iff α² + g²ω̃_max² < 1 and unstable if > 1 (normal linearization, X5 Lemma 5; the identification of the local branch with the actual update uses X5 Lemma 4 and Proposition 2 [ref]).
(iii) [Common latent Fisher] If G_a = G for all a, then S = G Ā_cyc G and DΦ = αI + gĀ_cycG on the realizable branch [ref: G⁻¹S = Ā_cycG, not GĀ_cyc; the two are similar and have the same spectrum]: the master-recursion Jacobian αI + gCJ* with (C, J*) replaced by (Ā_cyc, G). Hence the latent frontier reads α² + (g·ω_max(G^{1/2}Ā_cycG^{1/2}))² < 1.
(iv) [Induced potential and the X3 mechanism] The pooled directional forcing on θ is β Σ_a w_a Φ̃_{a,attr}ᵀJ_a u_a with u_a = Φ̃_{a,attr}(ū_lat + Ā_cycφ̄_{a,attr}). θ = 0 is a realizable fixed point iff ū_lat + Ā_cycφ̄_{a,attr} ∈ ker Φ̃_{a,attr} for every a (in particular when every panel is centered and ū_lat = 0, which gives u_a = 0 for all a), and it is stationary for the round iff Σ_a w_a Φ̃_{a,attr}ᵀu_a = 0, the exact latent form of the aggregate cancellation in X3 [ref]. In the realizable case (ii)–(iii) apply at θ* = 0. Nonvanishing aggregate potentials move the equilibrium toward concentration and reduce the realized gain (numerically: Section 5).

*Proof.* (i) is X5 Theorem 1 with B_a = Φ_{a,attr}; the latent form of S uses J_aΠ_a = J_a (J_a1 = 0, symmetric) and C_a = Π_aΦ_aĀΦ_aᵀΠ_a (Proposition L1), so Φ_{a,attr}ᵀJ_aC_aJ_aΦ_{a,attr} = (Φ_{a,attr}ᵀJ_aΦ_a)Ā(Φ_aᵀJ_aΦ_{a,attr}); the intercept coordinate of Φ_a is annihilated by J_a (J_a1 = 0), so Φ_aᵀJ_aΦ_{a,attr} = G_a padded, and only Ā_cyc survives. (ii) and (iii) are X5 Theorem 3 and direct substitution. (iv) The closed form of u_a is Proposition L1(iv). With u_a = 0 and C_a1 = 0, F_a(0) = 0 (the coverage and sampler terms both multiply C_a1/K_a), so z_a = 0 for all a at θ = 0, and the inner objective Σ w_a KL(softmax(Φ_{a,attr}θ)‖softmax(0)) is minimized exactly where every softmax(Φ_{a,attr}θ) is uniform, i.e. Φ̃_{a,attr}θ = 0 for all a, i.e. θ = 0 under (H1); hence θ* = 0 is a fixed point with e_a = 0, R = 0. The realizability and stationarity conditions follow by evaluating u_a and the gradient Σ_a w_a Φ̃_{a,attr}ᵀJ_a(x_a − z_a) at θ = 0 (where J_a = Π_a/K_a). ∎

Remarks. (a) The partial-sharing round corresponds to φ = [φ_attr; 1_{group}(a) ⊗ φ_attr] with a ridge on the second block; the ridge adds ρP to the Hessian, so it is an extension of (i), not a special case, and its coupling matrix (J_a + ρΠ)⁻¹J_a is stated in the partial-sharing note [ref]. (b) Nothing is claimed about nonlinear features, sampled losses, or the reward model's estimation error; Ā is treated as known (identified) when the dynamics are run.

## 5. Numerical checks (`latent_check.py`; m = 4: three attributes plus intercept; latent cycle Ā_cyc = 0.1·R_cyc; α = 0.9, λ = 1)

Seed 20261013; run with `python3 latent_check.py` (about 15 minutes, dominated by the exact inner Newton solves in the simulations).

**(1) Identifiability (Proposition L2).** Dense random features, 60 prompts with one labeled pair each: design rank 6 of 6, Ā recovered with max entry error 2.0·10⁻¹⁶. Role-plus-intercept features (the sparse toy, 3 prompts, 3 observed role pairs): design rank 3 of 6; the minimum-norm solution happens to return the cycle block to 2.8·10⁻¹⁷ with a zero intercept column, but this is an artifact of the solver's choice within the null space [ref]: each observation is Ā_rs + ū_r − ū_s, so the cycle block and the latent potential are confounded and only their three observed combinations are identified. So with role features only the observed pairs are learned; with features in general position the whole form is.

**(2) Per-prompt blindness (Proposition L1).** Random panels in the 3-dimensional attribute space, pure latent cycle (latent cyclic share 1.000): mean cyclic energy share of the induced panel flow 0.000 (K_a = 2), 0.304 (K_a = 3), 0.509 (K_a = 4), 0.667 (K_a = 6), over 200 panels each (2000 draws: 0.334, 0.514, 0.668, consistent with the conjecture (K−2)/K for standard-normal attributes; the shares fall when the attribute distribution has a nonzero mean) [ref]. A pair sees nothing; larger panels see more of the cycle.

**(3) Latent dynamics (Theorem L3).** N = 6 prompts, K_a = 4 generated candidates each with random attribute features (standard normal, then centered per panel), Ā_cyc = 0.1·R_cyc, no latent potential, α = 0.9, λ = 1, shared θ ∈ R³. [ref] The first run used an inner Newton step that could accept non-descent directions when the Hessian is indefinite and then stall; the rows below come from the replaced solver (damped Newton with Armijo backtracking and a stationarity assertion on every return).

*Centered panels (φ̄_a = 0).* Induced potentials u_a = 0 and every panel exposes the full cycle (cyclic share 1.000). θ* = 0 is the fixed point (|θ*| = 2·10⁻¹⁶), R = 0, and the latent form of S matches Σ w_a B_aᵀJ_aC_aJ_aB_a to 7·10⁻¹⁸. The Jacobian formula and central finite differences of the exact round agree to 4 decimals at every β. Spectrum: G⁻¹ᐟ²SG⁻¹ᐟ² has eigenvalues ±0.0797i and 0, so the multipliers are α ± 0.0797iβ and the latent frontier is β* = √(1−α²)/0.0797 = 5.47 [ref].

The independent referee run (its own solver, 2000 rounds) gives [1.65, 2.46], [2.07, 3.32] and a concentrated endpoint with ‖θ‖ ≈ 153 at β = 24; the tail norm of a concentrated orbit depends on the horizon and solver tolerances, the concentration itself does not. So the shared policy is bounded nonconvergent for 8 ≤ β ≤ 16 and collapses to a deterministic winner on every panel at β = 24, even though u_a = 0 for all a: the latent cycle alone can also end in concentration once the gain is large enough, as in the manuscript's endpoint dichotomy for a single prompt.

Note that with K_a = 4 centered panels several prompts are already unstable in isolation; this design tests the latent frontier, not the isolated-versus-shared dichotomy (which is the subject of X3 and of the partial-sharing note).

*Offset panels (independent random offsets of size c = 0.5 per panel, rerun with the repaired solver).* Induced potentials |u_a| between 0.06 and 0.16 and cyclic shares between 0.07 and 0.89. At β = 4 the orbit converges to a nonzero fixed point (radius 0.9404, ‖θ*‖ = 1.04). For β = 8, 12, 16, 24 the interior fixed point found by Newton is unstable (radius 1.021, 1.138, 1.278, 1.585; formula = FD; ‖R‖ = 0.03–0.07), but the simulated orbit does not stay near it: ‖θ‖ settles at 10.7, 44.1, 58.2, 84.4 and the smallest top probability over panels rises to 0.82–0.95, i.e. the shared policy concentrates. (The c = 1.0 rows of the first run, which reported collapse with ‖θ‖ up to 1274 from a stalled fixed-point search, are withdrawn and were not rerun; they add nothing beyond c = 0.5 [ref].) This is the latent form of the X3 mechanism in reverse: panel offsets act as prompt-specific latent potentials (Remark (b) to Proposition L1), their aggregate is not cancelled, the policy collapses toward its winners, and the latent cycle is damped by the vanishing Fisher mass. Together with the centered β = 24 row, the dynamics are bounded nonconvergence versus concentration, as in the manuscript's endpoint dichotomy; which one occurs is decided by the gain and the aggregate induced potential, not by the latent cycle alone.
